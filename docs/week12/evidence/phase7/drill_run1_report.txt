4. WHAT HAPPENED AND ROOT CAUSE, AS FAR AS THE TRACE SHOWS
- On 10-30 from about 13:32 to 14:52 the MCP server did not answer (30 s timeouts). The engine silently fell back to local retrieval, which is the "fallback" in the flags.
- On those requests the code validator had no symbol source (`symbol_source=none`, `lookups=0`). It could not check whether the generated method is deprecated or exists in the caller's version. It recorded the tier `not_checked`, counted zero hard and zero soft failures, and let the answer through.
- So any deprecated method in those 4 samples would have been shipped unflagged. This is the one situation where the engine could not detect the problem it exists to prevent.
- I cannot confirm any of the 4 samples actually contained a deprecated method. The logs hold no code or symbol names. This is a risk exposure that fits the complaint, not a proven match.
- The 13 deprecated-tier requests are ambiguous:
  - All 23 look identical, with hard=0 and soft=0.
  - They occur at a steady rate from 10-22 onward, so no event separates last week from earlier.
  - They may be the validator correctly recognising and handling a deprecated symbol, for example by annotating it.
  - They may instead be deprecated methods passing through with no failure counted.
  - The trace does not say which. I judged them more likely baseline than the incident, but with low confidence.

6. TOOL FEEDBACK
Good:
- `--code-tier` and the `flags` column made the outage cluster visible quickly.
- `find` output with fallback and code flags was readable.

Slow, confusing or missing:
- `find` cannot filter on `fallback`, `mcp_fallback` or error category. I had to filter `find` output with grep to see the fallback cluster.
- The `deprecated` tier is ambiguous. The output never says whether the deprecated symbol was warned about or stripped. The `soft` and `hard` counts stay 0 for these requests, so I cannot tell a handled deprecation from a missed one.
- Retrieval and the fallback line are hidden in the spans: `tools=-` and the `fallback:N` flag are cryptic. Nothing names an outage.
- There is no way to group `find` results by time or by flag. Spotting the 10-30 outage cluster took multiple greps.
- `--since 14d` is relative to the real clock, not to the drill date. The synthetic data is dated Oct-Nov 2026, so absolute dates were safer.
- `show --json` carries no extra data, such as symbol names or a deprecation verdict.

Single change that would have helped most: record the specific validator verdict on `code_validation`. For example, a count of symbols found deprecated and whether a warning was emitted, and a loud `code_unchecked_reason=mcp_timeout` on not_checked requests. Pair that with a `find --unchecked` or `--fallback` filter. With that, the not_checked requests would not need a separate hypothesis, and deprecated-tier requests could be told apart as warned or silent.
